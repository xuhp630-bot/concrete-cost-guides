# How Many Bags of Concrete Do You Need for a 10x10 Slab?

Pouring a 10x10 concrete slab is one of the most common DIY projects for homeowners — but getting the material calculation right before you start is critical. Too few bags and you're making emergency trips to the hardware store mid-pour. Too many and you're wasting money on materials you'll never use.

## The Quick Answer

For a standard 10x10 slab at 4 inches thick, you'll need approximately **56 bags of 80 lb concrete** or around **74 bags of 60 lb concrete**. This assumes no waste and perfect conditions — in practice, most contractors recommend adding 10% extra.

## How the Calculation Works

Concrete volume is measured in cubic yards. Here's the formula:

Length (ft) × Width (ft) × Depth (ft) ÷ 27 = Cubic Yards

For a 10x10 slab at 4 inches (0.33 feet) thick:

10 × 10 × 0.33 ÷ 27 = **1.22 cubic yards**

Each 80 lb bag of Quikrete or Sakrete yields approximately 0.022 cubic yards. So:

1.22 ÷ 0.022 = **55.5 bags → round up to 56**

For 60 lb bags (0.0165 cubic yards each):

1.22 ÷ 0.0165 = **73.9 bags → round up to 74**

## 4-Inch vs 6-Inch Slab: Does Thickness Matter?

Yes — significantly. A 6-inch slab for a driveway or workshop floor uses 50% more concrete than a 4-inch patio slab. For a 10x10 at 6 inches, you're looking at **84 bags of 80 lb** concrete.

## Bagged Concrete vs. Ready-Mix Delivery

For projects under 1 cubic yard, bagged concrete is usually more cost-effective. Once you're above 1-1.5 cubic yards, ready-mix delivery often makes more sense — both financially and logistically.

A good rule of thumb: if you need more than 60 bags, get a quote from a ready-mix supplier. The [concrete delivery cost calculator](https://concreteestimatorhub.com/concrete-delivery-cost-calculator) can help you compare the two options side by side.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

**Forgetting the waste factor.** Always order 10% more than your calculation. Spills, uneven subgrades, and form leaks all consume material.

**Mixing too fast.** For large pours, bagged concrete hardens quickly once mixed. Work in sections or use a mixer.

**Skipping the rebar.** For structural slabs — garages, driveways, sheds — 1/2 inch rebar on 18-inch centers adds critical tensile strength. It doesn't change your concrete volume calculation, but it's essential for longevity.
