# Stamped Concrete Patio Cost: What to Expect in 2026

Stamped concrete patios have become one of the most popular outdoor upgrades in recent years — and for good reason. They give homeowners the look of natural stone, brick, or slate at a fraction of the material cost. But before you commit to a project, understanding the real cost breakdown is essential.

## What Is Stamped Concrete?

Stamped concrete is regular concrete that gets textured and colored before it fully cures. Contractors press rubber stamps into the wet surface to create patterns that mimic natural materials. The most common patterns include:

- Ashlar slate (most popular for patios)
- Cobblestone and brick patterns
- Wood plank texture
- Random stone and flagstone

The result is a durable, low-maintenance surface that handles weather well and can last 25 years or more with proper sealing.

## How Much Does a Stamped Concrete Patio Cost?

For 2026, expect to pay between **$8 and $20 per square foot** installed, depending on your region, pattern complexity, and number of colors. Here is a breakdown by project size:

A 200 square foot patio (roughly 10x20 feet) typically costs $1,600 to $4,000. A 400 square foot patio (20x20 feet) runs $3,200 to $8,000. A 600 square foot patio with premium patterns can reach $12,000 or more.

## What Drives the Cost Up

**Pattern complexity** is the biggest variable. A simple single-color ashlar pattern sits at the lower end. Multi-color blending with intricate borders adds $3 to $6 per square foot to the base price.

**Color selection** matters more than most homeowners realize. A single integral color is included in most base quotes. Accent colors, antiquing agents, and release powder combinations each add cost but create a significantly more realistic appearance.

**Site preparation** accounts for 15 to 25 percent of total project cost. If your yard has poor drainage, slopes toward the house, or has existing concrete to remove, expect higher prep costs. The concrete itself needs a properly graded and compacted base — shortcuts here lead to cracking later.

**Sealing** is not optional for stamped concrete. A quality polyurethane or acrylic sealer every 2 to 3 years protects the color and surface texture. First-time sealing is typically included in the installation quote; ongoing maintenance is your responsibility.

## Stamped Concrete vs. Plain Concrete

The cost premium over plain concrete is real but often worth it. A standard broom-finished patio runs $6 to $10 per square foot installed. Stamped concrete adds $3 to $8 per square foot on top of that — primarily for the labor of stamping, coloring, and finishing.

What you get for that premium is significant: a surface that blends with landscaping, adds curb appeal, and increases perceived home value in ways plain gray concrete simply does not.

If you are comparing stamped concrete against natural flagstone or pavers, the math shifts. Natural flagstone typically costs $15 to $30 per square foot installed. Pavers run $12 to $25. Stamped concrete often wins on total cost while delivering a comparable look.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

**Skipping the expansion joints.** Concrete expands and contracts with temperature. Without properly placed saw-cut joints, your patio will develop random cracks that cut through the pattern. Good contractors plan joint placement to look intentional.

**Going too thin.** Patio slabs should be at least 4 inches thick. For areas that might see vehicle traffic or heavy furniture, 6 inches is safer. Thin slabs are the number one cause of premature cracking.

**Hiring based on price alone.** Stamped concrete requires timing and skill that plain concrete does not. The stamping window closes fast — typically 1 to 2 hours depending on temperature. Inexperienced crews rush the process and produce muddy patterns. Get references and see photos of previous work.
