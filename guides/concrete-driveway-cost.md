# Concrete Driveway Cost Calculator: What to Budget in 2026

A concrete driveway is one of the most practical investments a homeowner can make. Done right, it lasts 30 years or more with minimal maintenance. Done wrong, it cracks within five years. Getting the cost estimate right before you hire anyone is the first step to doing it right.

## Average Concrete Driveway Cost in 2026

Nationally, concrete driveways run between $4 and $15 per square foot installed, with most projects landing in the $6 to $10 range. For a standard two-car driveway of 400 to 500 square feet, that works out to $2,400 to $5,000 installed.

The wide range exists because driveway costs depend on several factors: your region's labor costs, the thickness of the slab, any special finishes, and site-specific prep work.

## The Four Main Cost Drivers

**Slab thickness** is the single biggest material variable. A residential driveway for passenger vehicles typically uses a 4-inch slab. If you plan to park heavy trucks, RVs, or construction equipment, upgrade to 6 inches — this adds roughly 50% more concrete volume and 20 to 30% to your total cost.

**Site preparation** can double your quote if the conditions are bad. Removing an existing asphalt or concrete driveway typically costs $1,000 to $3,000 depending on size. Regrading a sloped or poorly drained area adds another $500 to $2,000. These aren't optional extras — skipping them leads to premature cracking.

**Finish type** changes both cost and durability. Standard broom finish is the baseline at $4 to $7 per square foot. Exposed aggregate adds $2 to $4. Stamped patterns add $4 to $8. Colored concrete adds $1 to $3. Each upgrade is legitimate but needs to be factored into your total budget from the start.

**Rebar or fiber reinforcement** is non-negotiable for a driveway. Unlike a backyard patio that only holds furniture weight, a driveway bears vehicle loads and thermal expansion stress year-round. Half-inch rebar on 18-inch centers costs $0.50 to $1.00 per square foot and is worth every dollar.

## Driveway Extension: When You Need More Space

If your existing driveway is functional but undersized, extending it is usually cheaper than full replacement. A concrete driveway extension runs $6 to $12 per square foot, partly because smaller pours require proportionally more setup cost.

## Concrete vs. Asphalt: The Real Cost Comparison

Asphalt typically costs $3 to $7 per square foot installed — cheaper upfront than concrete. But asphalt requires resealing every 3 to 5 years ($0.15 to $0.25 per square foot) and full replacement in 15 to 20 years. Concrete costs more initially but lasts twice as long with almost no maintenance.

Over a 30-year horizon, the total cost of ownership often favors concrete by a significant margin, especially in climates where asphalt softens in summer heat.

## Getting Quotes You Can Actually Compare

Before calling contractors, know your square footage, desired thickness, and whether you need any demolition or grading. These three numbers let you compare quotes on equal terms. Without them, contractors can build in margins you won't catch until your project is half done.

A reliable estimate from an online tool puts you in a much stronger negotiating position. Run the numbers first, then get three contractor bids and compare them against your baseline.
